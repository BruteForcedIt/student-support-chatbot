# Anti-Harassment and Sexual Misconduct

UNM prohibits all forms of sexual harassment and misconduct on its premises, including the Nottingham Future Students Centre. The policy covers students, staff and third parties working with the university. People are encouraged to report incidents formally, with any evidence, as early as possible. Under Malaysian law, consequences for perpetrators can include disciplinary action, suspension, dismissal and legal action.

## Forms of sexual harassment recognised
- Verbal: sexually suggestive comments, jokes, sounds or questions.
- Non-verbal or gestural: sexually suggestive gestures or body language.
- Visual: showing sexually explicit images or videos.
- Psychological: behaviour that causes psychological distress or discomfort.
- Physical: unwanted touching, patting, pinching, stroking, hugging or kissing.

## How to report
- Students: use the Student Complaint Procedure (https://www.nottingham.edu.my/CurrentStudents/StudentRegistry/Complaints.aspx) or email Student.Complaints@nottingham.edu.my. Complaints are handled under the Handling of Student Sexual Harassment Procedure.
- Complaints about staff members: email HR at Staff.Grievance@nottingham.edu.my (UNM Grievance Procedure).

## What happens after a report
- The Student Conduct and Appeals Officer (SCAO) receives student complaints and does a first assessment.
- A Review Panel carries out the preliminary investigation. It can dismiss the complaint, propose mediation, refer it to the University Assessor with recommendations, or ask for further investigation.
- The person reporting is offered support by the SCAO, a check for any protective measures needed, and information on options, including going to the police or asking for a university investigation, and on specialist support.
- If a student is convicted of a related criminal offence or accepts a police caution, the case is normally referred to a Disciplinary Committee. The university can still act under its Code of Discipline even if the police take no action or a trial ends in acquittal.

## Confidentiality
Reporters keep as much control as possible over the process. If a report identifies the people involved and suggests an urgent risk to the reporter or others, the university may need to act even without a request, but will consult the reporter first wherever possible. Records are kept according to the university's retention policy.

## False reports
Knowingly making a false accusation or giving misleading information breaks university policy and may lead to serious disciplinary and legal action.
